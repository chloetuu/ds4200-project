Analyzing Sector Performance and Top S&P 500 Stocks

This project analyzes the performance of selected S&P 500 stocks and sector ETFs from 2022 to 2025, a period defined by the rise of AI-driven investment. As companies like NVIDIA, AMD, and Meta emerged as central players in the broader economy, traditional sector relationships began to break down. Our goal is to move from a broad view of sector-level trends down to the behavior of individual stocks, using a combination of static and interactive visualizations to uncover how concentration, volatility, and market sensitivity evolved over this critical period.

Dataset Overview

The dataset was collected using the yfinance Python library, which retrieves historical financial data from Yahoo Finance. It includes daily OHLCV data (open, high, low, close, adjusted close, and trading volume) for selected S&P 500 stocks and sector ETFs spanning 2022 to 2025. Additional engineered features include daily returns, rolling volatility (7-day and 30-day), moving averages, momentum indicators, and rolling beta relative to SPY. The dataset contains over 10,000 observations and more than 12 features, including both continuous variables (price, return, beta) and categorical attributes (sector, ticker type). Stocks analyzed include NVDA, AAPL, MSFT, AMD, META, GOOG, AMZN, TSLA, JPM, XOM, and LLY, alongside 10 sector ETFs and the SPY market benchmark.

Visualization 1: Sector ETF Monthly Return Heatmap

🖱️ How to interact: Hover over any cell to see the exact sector, month, and return value in a tooltip.

Takeaway: The heatmap reveals that the 2022 bear market hit nearly every sector simultaneously — visible as a widespread red band across all rows. The recovery from mid-2023 onward was strikingly uneven: Technology and Consumer Discretionary sectors showed consistent green returns while Real Estate and Utilities remained largely flat or negative. This pattern indicates that AI-related industries were the primary engine of recent market strength, not broad economic recovery.

Visualization 2: Top Stocks by Sector Total Return

🖱️ How to interact: Use the sector dropdown to switch between sectors and see the top-performing stocks update instantly. Hover over any bar for the stock name and exact return percentage.

Takeaway: Across every sector, returns are heavily concentrated in one or two names rather than evenly distributed. In Technology, NVDA's ~660% return dwarfs AMD (~89%), MSFT (~60%), and AAPL (~58%) — a gap so extreme it suggests passive tech exposure carries significant single-stock risk. In Health Care, Eli Lilly similarly dominates. This concentration pattern reinforces that sector-level ETF returns are driven by outliers, not the average constituent.

Visualization 3: Stock Candlestick Chart

🖱️ How to interact: Hover over any candle to see the open, high, low, and close prices. Click and drag to zoom into a specific time range. Double-click to reset. Use the Plotly toolbar (top right) to pan, zoom, or download.

Takeaway: The price climbing on the candlestick chart is of course interesting but let's focus more on the size of those daily wicks. You can see that each candle has its high and low, indicating the range that stock was able to swing within one day, and for something like NVDA those ranges are large. Hard evidence that despite 5-10 changes (upward & downward) on a daily basis, the net positive emerged through some turbulence along the way. And that tells you just because your returns are high doesn't mean it was a smooth ride. Looking at something like JPM or XOM compared to them & the candles are tighter, and therefore day-to-day action is much more predictable. Meaning that if you were long NVDA in 2023 and 2024, you were collecting those returns at the expense of a couple of super rough days.

Visualization 4: 30-Day Rolling Beta vs SPY

🖱️ How to interact: Type any ticker (e.g. NVDA, AAPL, TSLA, META) in the search box or select from the autocomplete dropdown, then click Load to update the chart. Hover over the line for exact date and beta values. Available tickers: NVDA, AAPL, MSFT, AMD, META, GOOG, AMZN, TSLA, JPM, XOM, LLY, SPY.

📖 What is Beta? Beta measures how much a stock moves relative to the broader market (SPY). A beta of 1 means the stock moves in line with the market. Above 1 means it amplifies market moves, so a beta of 3 means if the S&P 500 drops 1%, that stock tends to drop 3%. Rolling beta shows how this sensitivity changes over time, which is key for understanding risk.

Takeaway: This chart tells the story of a fundamental shift in how tech stocks relate to the rest of the market. Historically, even high-growth stocks eventually stabilize, but the data here shows the opposite happening as AI took over. You can see the rolling beta for names like NVDA and AMD consistently spiking well above 2.0 or even 3.0, meaning they were moving with triple the intensity of the S&P 500. Comparing this to a defensive staple like XOM or JPM, which stayed anchored near 1.0, makes the story clear: the "AI trade" fundamentally rewired these stocks into market amplifiers, where the price of massive returns was a permanent increase in sensitivity to every macro swing.

Visualization 5: Interactive Sector Trends Explorer (D3)

🖱️ How to interact: Hover over any line to highlight it and see the sector name, date, and index value in a tooltip. Click any sector name in the legend to hide or show that line — isolating specific sectors for direct comparison. Click again to bring it back.

Takeaway: Technology diverged sharply from all other sectors starting in early 2023 and never looked back. Defensive sectors like Utilities, Consumer Staples, and Real Estate barely recovered to their 2022 starting levels by 2025. This visualization ties the whole project together — the sector-level divergence seen here is the structural backdrop that drove NVDA's outsized individual returns shown above. Try isolating Technology vs Real Estate in the legend for the starkest comparison.

Key Findings and Conclusion

Three findings stand out from this analysis. First, the post-2023 market was not broadly strong — it was narrow, driven almost entirely by AI-exposed technology stocks while defensive sectors like Real Estate and Utilities remained flat or negative in real terms.

Second, concentration within sectors is extreme: NVDA alone accounts for a disproportionate share of Technology sector returns, meaning passive tech exposure carries significant single-stock risk. The same pattern holds in Health Care (LLY) and Communication Services (META).

Third, AI enthusiasm amplified risk rather than reducing it: NVDA's persistently high and rising beta shows that as its weight in indices grew, it became more sensitive to market swings — not less. High-growth AI stocks offer extraordinary returns alongside extraordinary volatility, underscoring the importance of diversification.

Future work could extend this analysis through 2026, incorporate macroeconomic indicators such as Fed rate decisions and CPI as event markers, and broaden the individual stock analysis beyond Technology to test whether similar concentration patterns exist in Energy or Financials.
